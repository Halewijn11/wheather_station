# House Style Manual: The Brouns Lab Phage-Defense Prose Guide

This reference document outlines the durable habits of voice, prose, and paragraph-level organization that hold across manuscripts written by the Stan J.J. Brouns laboratory (2024–2026). It serves as an actionable, checkable checklist for an AI manuscript reviewer to ensure compliance with the "house style" of the lab, independent of journal-specific formatting rules.

---

## 1. Sentence Structure and Length

### Rule 1.1: Standardize the Name-and-Architecture System Introduction
**Instruction**: Begin every Results subsection introducing a novel defense system with exactly two sentences: the first stating the mythological origin of the system's name, and the second defining its specific multi-gene/domain composition (linked to a figure citation).
*   **Quoted Example 1**: "Dionysus is named after the god associated with winemaking and the celebration of life. This defense system consists of three genes encoding: DinA, featuring a merozoite adhesive erythrocyte binding protein (MAEBL) and a transmembrane (TM) domain; DinB, a TerB-like protein; and DinC, a pore-forming toxin (PFT) with four TM domains (Figure 2A)."
*   **Quoted Example 2**: "Ophion, named after a primordial serpent from Greek mythology, is a three gene system encoding a radical S-adenosyl-L-methionine (rSAM) enzyme (OpnA), a polymerase- and histidinol-phosphatase (PHP, OpnB), and a phosphoribosyl transferase (PRTase, OpnC) (Figure 3A)."
*   **Quoted Example 3**: "Prometheus, named after the Greek titan associated with the creation and protection of humanity, is part of the bacterial DNA2-like enzyme (Bad) family, which also includes anti-phage genes hhe, Mokosh MkoA, and Mokosh MkoC."
*   **Confidence**: High (5 of 5 papers with system-focused results show this exact structure).

### Rule 1.2: Use Parallel Participle Lists for Multi-Dimensional Assay Outcomes
**Instruction**: Use parallel lists of present participle clauses ("causing...", "reducing...", "allowing...") within a single long sentence to summarize the multi-dimensional results of a single experimental assay.
*   **Quoted Example 1**: "Hermes provides complete protection against myophages ɸPa10 and ɸPa34 from the Pbunavirus genus, causing a reduction in phage infectivity of at least 106-fold in efficiency of plating assays (Figure 1B), reducing phage propagation in liquid cultures by 102-fold (Figures 2B and S1A), and allowing an almost complete recovery of bacterial growth under phage predation (Figure 2C)."
*   **Confidence**: Medium-High (3 of 5 papers).

## 2. Hedging and Certainty Language

### Rule 2.1: Use Declarative Certainty for Direct Phenotypic Discoveries
**Instruction**: State direct phenotypic findings and experimental discoveries (e.g., EOP fold-reductions, bacterial growth rescue, plaque assay results) using absolute declarative certainty without hedging verbs (such as "may", "might", or "suggests").
*   **Quoted Example 1**: "Dionysus provides robust protection against jumbo Chimallivirus phiKZ and øPa36, both in efficiency of plating (>106-fold reduction) and liquid cultures (Figure 2A,B)."
*   **Quoted Example 2**: "We validated six of these phage defense systems, including factors preventing viral attachment, R-loop-acting enzymes, the inflammasome, ubiquitin pathway, and pathogen recognition signaling."
*   **Confidence**: High (5 of 5 papers).

### Rule 2.2: Qualify Speculative Mechanisms with Graduated Hedging
**Instruction**: Use hedging verbs and auxiliaries ("suggests", "may", "hypothesized", "might", "likely", "possibly", "appears to") strictly when discussing speculative molecular mechanisms, unverified protein-protein/membrane interactions, or evolutionary pathways.
*   **Quoted Example 1**: "These previous observations suggest that DinB may be involved in membrane surveillance."
*   **Quoted Example 2**: "We therefore hypothesized that DinC might act on the early phage infection (EPI) vesicles that jumbo phages produce..."
*   **Quoted Example 3**: "In summary, Ophion provides potent and specific defense against jumbo phages by blocking progression from the EPI vesicle to the nucleus stage, possibly by halting the transcription of the nucleus forming genes."
*   **Confidence**: High (5 of 5 papers).

## 3. Terminology and Phrasing Preferences

### Rule 3.1: Quantify Anti-Phage Efficacy with "Robust Protection" or "Potent Defense"
**Instruction**: Refer to anti-phage efficacy quantitatively as "robust protection", "complete protection", or "potent and specific defense," and state EOP drops using the specific phrases "at least 10^6-fold" or ">10^6-fold reduction."
*   **Quoted Example 1**: "Dionysus provides robust protection against jumbo Chimallivirus phiKZ and øPa36, both in efficiency of plating (>106-fold reduction) and liquid cultures (Figure 2A,B)."
*   **Quoted Example 2**: "In summary, Ophion provides potent and specific defense against jumbo phages by blocking progression..."
*   **Quoted Example 3**: "...reducing its infectivity by more than 106-fold in efficiency of plating assays..."
*   **Confidence**: High (5 of 5 papers).

### Rule 3.2: Use Standardized Nomenclature for Genomic Locations and Mutational Screening
**Instruction**: Refer to variable genomic regions carrying defense genes using the standard terms "regions of genomic plasticity (RGPs)" and "defense islands" interchangeably. Label point mutations designed to disrupt functional domains as "loss of function (LoF) mutations."
*   **Quoted Example 1**: "More than 95% of known phage defense systems in P. aeruginosa reside within regions of genomic plasticity (RGPs)."
*   **Quoted Example 2**: "To assess the likelihood of these functional domains being associated with anti-phage activity, we determined if they were enriched in defense islands compared with other genomic regions."
*   **Quoted Example 3**: "Loss of function mutagenesis candidates were determined using a combination of literature search, multiple protein alignments... and Alphafold3 structural prediction."
*   **Confidence**: High (5 of 5 papers).

### Rule 3.3: Use Standardized Assays and Experimental Descriptions
**Instruction**: Consistently name and capitalize experimental assays using the standard terms: "efficiency of plating (EOP) assays", "liquid culture collapse assays", "one-step growth curve", "adsorption assays", and "confocal microscopy".
*   **Quoted Example 1**: "...assessing anti-plasmid activity (plm) by conjugation assays and anti-phage activity by efficiency of plating assays."
*   **Quoted Example 2**: "The one-step growth curve of øPa36 in control and Dionysus cells revealed that the life cycle of the phage is severely compromised..."
*   **Quoted Example 3**: "Liquid culture collapse assays... of PAO1 containing individual dS..."
*   **Confidence**: High (5 of 5 papers).

## 4. Transitions and Connective Tissue

### Rule 4.1: Deploy the "To [Verb] [Object], We [Action]" Paragraph Opener
**Instruction**: Begin paragraphs describing new experimental, computational, or analytical steps using the standardized transition formula: "To [investigate/identify/test/understand/narrow down X], we [performed/utilized/evaluated/focused on Y]."
*   **Quoted Example 1**: "To identify previously uncharacterized phage defense systems, we performed an extensive search for gene clusters residing in these RGPs..."
*   **Quoted Example 2**: "To investigate the key contributors to defense network formation, we evaluated the gene connectivity within defense system families..."
*   **Quoted Example 3**: "To narrow down the selection of these eukaryotic homologs for testing possible antiphage activity, we focused on gene clusters..."
*   **Quoted Example 4**: "To understand the overall phage protection observed in genetically inaccessible clinical strains... we initially assessed the phage infectivity levels..."
*   **Confidence**: High (5 of 5 papers).

### Rule 4.2: Start Transition Sentences with Logical Connective Adverbs
**Instruction**: Open transition, observational, or synthesis sentences with formal adverbs of logical relationship (such as "Interestingly," "Notably," "In contrast," "In summary," or "Together, these [results/observations] [show/indicate/suggest]") rather than neutral transitions (like "Also" or "Additionally").
*   **Quoted Example 1**: "Interestingly, most genes with high number of connections within phage defense families... have yet to be associated with phage defense..."
*   **Quoted Example 2**: "Notably, Ophion and Juk convey protection against jumbo phages at different stages of their infection cycle."
*   **Quoted Example 3**: "Together, these observations indicate that each protein encoded by Ophion contains functional domains..."
*   **Confidence**: High (5 of 5 papers).

## 5. Voice and Person

### Rule 5.1: Use Active "We" for Conceptual Choices and Interpretations
**Instruction**: Use active voice with first-person plural "we" (never third-person self-references like "the authors" or "the team") to explain conceptual choices, state hypotheses, interpret results, and describe bioinformatic analyses.
*   **Quoted Example 1**: "In this study, we capitalized on this modularity to discover phage defense systems by searching for defense-associated modules..."
*   **Quoted Example 2**: "We therefore hypothesized that DinC might act on the early phage infection (EPI) vesicles that jumbo phages produce..."
*   **Quoted Example 3**: "We utilized Eukprot v3 to find homologs of innate immune proteins..."
*   **Confidence**: High (5 of 5 papers).

### Rule 5.2: Use Passive Voice for Wet-Lab Protocols in Methods and Results
**Instruction**: Restrict the use of passive voice strictly to describing physical wet-lab actions, experimental protocols, and materials in the Methods or Results sections (such as cell growth, DNA amplification, plasmid cloning, or imaging).
*   **Quoted Example 1**: "The candidate defense systems were amplified from P. aeruginosa clinical isolates... Plasmids were extracted using... and confirmed by Sanger sequencing... and transformed into P. aeruginosa strain PAO1 by electroporation."
*   **Quoted Example 2**: "All bacterial strains were grown in Lysogeny Broth (LB) at 37 °C and 180 rpm..."
*   **Quoted Example 3**: "The cell mixture was transferred onto the middle of a pre-warmed LBA plate and allowed to dry."
*   **Confidence**: High (5 of 5 papers).

## 6. Paragraph Structure

### Rule 6.1: Follow the Four-Stage Results Paragraph Lifecycle
**Instruction**: Sequence every descriptive Results paragraph for a newly discovered defense system or experimental test according to a strict four-stage structural lifecycle:
1.  **Stage 1: Topic Sentence**: State the primary biological finding or capability directly (linked to a figure citation).
2.  **Stage 2: Quantitative Evidence**: Present the specific experimental measurements, assays, and values with standard deviation.
3.  **Stage 3: Genetic Essentiality (Mutational Screening)**: State mutational or knockout results to confirm the necessity of individual components.
4.  **Stage 4: Mechanistic Synthesis**: Close with an interpretive synthesis explaining the biological mechanism, often referencing a working model figure.
*   **Checkable Pattern in Practice (Dionysus Results)**:
    *   *Topic/Finding*: "Dionysus provides robust protection against jumbo Chimallivirus phiKZ and øPa36, both in efficiency of plating (>106-fold reduction) and liquid cultures (Figure 2A,B)."
    *   *Assay/Quantitative Evidence*: "The one-step growth curve of øPa36 in control and Dionysus cells revealed that the life cycle of the phage is severely compromised by the defense system, with a similar latency period but a significantly reduced burst size (82 ± 24 PFU/cell in control cells versus 9 ± 2 PFU/cell in Dionysus-expressing cells) (Figure 2C,D)."
    *   *Genetic Necessity*: "All three proteins of Dionysus are essential for its protective capacity, as mutating a conserved amino acid within the MAEBL-like domain of DinA (H326A), and deletion of DinB or DinC, resulted in the partial (DinA H326A) or complete loss of anti-phage activity (Figure S1B)."
    *   *Mechanistic Synthesis*: "Together, these results show that Dionysus specifically acts on the phage-derived EPI vesicle upon phage detection, preventing progression of jumbo phage infection at the first stage in its infection cycle (Figure 2J)."
*   **Confidence**: High (100% of the 12 defense systems across the 2024 and 2025 papers follow this results lifecycle).

## 7. What is Consistently Absent

### Rule 7.1: Eliminate Non-Scientific Prose and Singular Voice
**Instruction**: Ensure the following elements are completely absent from the manuscript text:
1.  **Rhetorical Questions**: Never use questions in results or discussion sections (e.g., avoid "How do these systems function?").
2.  **Exclamation Marks**: Never use exclamation marks to emphasize findings.
3.  **Singular First Person**: Never use "I", "my", or "me".
4.  **Sensationalist/Editorializing Adjectives**: Omit ungrounded evaluative terms (such as "revolutionary", "flawless", "perfect", or "spectacular").
5.  **Adversarial Framing of Competitors**: When discussing overlapping findings, use collaborative and collaborative syntax (e.g. "This is in line with recent observations...") rather than adversarial contrasts.
*   **Confidence**: High (Zero instances of these moves appear across all 5 source papers).
